title: "What's actually true"
description: "A calm, source-linked brief and a local check for repeated social posts."

The **What's actually true** dashboard panel shows up to five current reports
that meet the family confidence policy. It aims for three to five lines, but
shows fewer when there is less evidence. It never fills gaps with invented claims.
Open it from Settings → Panels or search for “family” in the command palette.

## Confidence labels

Each news-feed story carries one of three labels:

- **Confirmed:** a matching publication from an explicitly recognized official
  source, linked directly to its official domain.
- **Credible:** matching reports from at least two distinct publisher families
  among AP, Reuters, The Washington Post, The New York Times and BBC.
- **Unconfirmed:** the loaded reports do not meet either rule. This does not mean
  the claim is false. A single respected outlet alone does not meet this policy.

The evaluator checks both the feed identity and destination URL. An aggregator
link, an official-looking display name, a generic credibility score, or a mention
of an agency in the headline cannot establish confirmation. Multiple editions of
one publisher count once. Headline matching is deliberately conservative;
differently worded coverage may remain Unconfirmed. The explicit source policy
lives in `src/config/family-facts.ts`.

“Confirmed” identifies an official publication; it is not an independent audit
of every claim or implication. Read the linked source before sharing. The brief
uses source headlines rather than an LLM-generated paraphrase.

## Pattern alerts

The family alert requires separate qualifying disruption reports in at least two
of aviation, banking/finance, telecom, cyber and power within the past six hours.
A single story cannot supply both domains. A single headline never triggers a
news banner. Emergency sirens remain a separate, unchanged safety signal.

The current classifier uses conservative English headline patterns. Unknown,
future or synthesized publication times are excluded. A fresh publication date
does not by itself establish a current disruption. Denials, hypothetical,
restored, historical and ambiguous reports are excluded. These rules can miss
real disruptions; they are not a complete operational monitoring service.

The brief's six-hour window expires every minute, including during failed feed
refreshes. Notification evaluation uses the complete committed news snapshot;
stale refreshes cannot send new pattern notifications. Browser pattern alerts
are silent and do not send email, Telegram or other external notifications.

Co-occurring reports do not establish a common cause. No alert is not an
all-clear: the result depends on selected feeds and available evidence.

## Check copied social posts

Expand **Check copied social posts** and paste a JSON array. The format example
is fictional. Analysis runs on this device, with no collection, upload or saved
post history. The dashboard's existing feeds do not supply account ages and
follower counts, so this is an explicit local import rather than live social
monitoring.

A flag requires at least five distinct accounts in one thirty-minute window.
Each account must be at most thirty days old when posting, or have fewer than
one hundred followers. The text must contain at least twelve words and share at
least ninety percent of its word triplets with every other member of the group.
Repeated posts from one account count once. Unknown profile fields stay
unassessed; supplied profile data is not independently verified.

Use `platform`, stable `accountId`, `text`, `postedAt`, optional
`accountCreatedAt`, optional `followers`, and optional `url`. Timestamps must
include a time zone or use epoch milliseconds. Omit unknown account fields or
set them to `null`. Input is limited to five hundred posts.

A flag establishes repeated wording, not falsehood, intent or coordination.
Legitimate announcements can be copied too. Check the underlying claim against
the linked official publications and reporting before sharing it.
